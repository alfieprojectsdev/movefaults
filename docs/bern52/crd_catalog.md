# CRD coordinate catalog

Generated by `scripts/build_crd_catalog.py` from **8,784 Bernese `.crd` files**
across `/srv/gnss-archive` and `$P`. Regenerate with the command in that
script's docstring; adding a root extends the catalog rather than replacing it.

**This is an identification index, not a coordinate product.** Its job is to
answer "which monument is this?" from an approximate position, to about 100 m —
the accuracy of a single-point position in a RINEX header. Frames and epochs
are deliberately not harmonised; see `SETTLED.md` for why.

## What the 2,703 rejects are

A total alone cannot distinguish genuine junk from one systematic parsing
fault — both look like 0.5%. Itemised:

| rows | reason |
|---|---|
| 1,824 | radius above Earth surface — solution diverged |
| 656 | radius below Earth surface — solution diverged |
| 223 | placeholder / LEO entry, all-zero coordinates |

Spread over **849 files**, of which only **12 are rejected entirely**. That
distribution is the answer: a parser fault would fail files uniformly, whereas
these are scattered bad rows plus a dozen wholesale failures.

`FN142881.CRD` is one of the twelve — a `GPSEST FINALL` run that did not
converge, putting all 52 of its stations ~600 km off at 491 km altitude. ALAB
there is 607,454 m from its catalog position. The radius check is rejecting a
bad file, not misreading a good one.

## What the GPS_1TB_2 drive added

The corpus went from 8,664 files to 8,784 when that drive was transferred in.
**2,195 site codes before, 2,195 after — not one new monument.** The 120 files
are further copies of coordinates the archive already held.

They are not inert, though. `n_files` rose on 441 sites, and because each site's
position is the **median** across its rows, adding rows moves that median
slightly: 145 sites shifted, by

| | |
|---|---|
| median shift | 0.0002 m |
| largest shift | 0.80 m (`KMNM`) |
| sites moved more than 1 m | **0** |

Three orders of magnitude below the ~100 m this catalog resolves, so nothing
about identification changes. Recorded because "the coordinates did not move"
would have been the easy claim and it is not true — checked column by column
across all 2,195 rows, not sampled.

## Ambiguous codes — 118 sites

A 4-char code can name genuinely different monuments across eras. These are
**not averaged**: the published row is the largest position cluster, and
`n_clusters` / `cluster_extent_m` say how far the others sit.

The worst is instructive. **CATA** is three monuments: two Philippine sites
~220 km apart, plus an Argentine station that appears in a global ITRF
reference file. A naive median would place CATA in empty ocean between them.
